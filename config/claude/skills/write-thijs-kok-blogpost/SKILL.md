## Before you start

If the topic, type (opinion or how-to), or angle isn't clear from the
request, ask before drafting. Useful questions:

- What's the topic, and what's the one thing the reader should take away?
- Opinion or how-to?
- Any existing notes, code, or a rough draft to work from?

## Rules

- Be succinct. Keep paragraphs short, two to four sentences. A single
  sentence paragraph is fine for emphasis or a transition.
- Use headers to break the post into logical sections. Each section covers
  one idea or aspect, not several.
- Use list items to break up long stretches of text when useful.
- Avoid hyphens as much as possible.
- Avoid marketing buzzwords: leverage, cutting edge, revolutionize,
  innovative, unlock, game changer, streamline, supercharge, next level,
  and similar.
- Back up statements with solid references (docs, source, a real example)
  rather than unsupported claims.

## Tone of voice

- First person, direct: "I decided to...", "I don't think...". Own the
  take instead of hedging it away.
- Plain, spoken register. Avoid academic transitions like "furthermore"
  or "in conclusion."
- Short, punchy sentences land the point after a longer explanatory one:
  "That's not a copyright problem. It's a reproducibility problem."
- An occasional dry, understated observation works well. Don't force it.
- Qualify predictions ("I wouldn't be surprised if...") rather than
  claiming certainty.

## Structure

- Title states the outcome or the thesis, not a vague topic label. One
  line under the title (dek/meta description) compresses what the reader
  gets out of it. Write this last.
- Open concrete: a scenario or the actual problem, never throat clearing.
- Section headers are concrete, not generic ("The Bug That Exists in Four
  Places" / "Checking Out the Stable Version"), never "Background" or
  "Conclusion."
- End with a real payoff: a reframed question, a compressed takeaway, or
  practical advice. Never a limp summary.

## Types

### Opinion / essay

AI, software industry shifts, engineering philosophy. Roughly 700 to 1100
words.

- Open on the common framing of a debate, then pivot with "But/Yet/Instead"
  into the sharper, less obvious question.
- Argue claim, then a concrete illustration ("Imagine...", "Suppose..."),
  then the point it reveals. Steelman the counter argument first.
- A cross domain analogy (e.g. music, photography) can help, but only use
  one when it actually clarifies the point. Don't force it.
- Recurring thematic core: as output gets cheaper, judgment, taste, and
  experience matter more, not less.

### How-to / technical

Code walkthroughs, tooling, infra setup. Length varies with the task,
don't pad a short fix into a long post.

- Open with the concrete problem and, briefly, the context that led there.
  Say why the naive or default approach falls short.
- Walk through the solution step by step or option by option, with real,
  runnable code, not pseudocode. A short, simple code example beats
  describing a mechanism in prose alone.
- When there's more than one valid approach, give each a header, a short
  Pros / Cons list, and a plain tradeoff sentence.
- Close with a short, practical recap of the tradeoff and what to pick, or
  for short posts, a brief warm sign off.
